# Base / alignment: Magee's own words, and why Square was anti-correlated (2026-09-28)

Agent 3 (CV / biomechanics research). Condensed. Primary source: Magee's
57-minute "Nothing But Net" video (YouTube ZMy9HGsoFAU), timestamps in brackets;
transcripts saved in the session scratchpad. Also four Championship Productions

## What Magee actually teaches (this corrects E19/E21)
- Shooting-side foot forward [15:39, 39:24, 46:03; lefties 52:05]. "Slightly"
  ahead - never quantified.
- Feet "about shoulder width" [46:30]. No number.
- Knees flexed, power from the legs [37:46, 39:37-39:44]; "get under the ball",
  "get connected" [36:29-37:31].
- Weight on the balls of the feet; back on the heels -> short / front rim
  [33:34, 37:56-38:12]. Come up AND FORWARD, not straight up [33:05, 38:25].
  Jumping over the three-point line is fine [34:18].
- SQUARE: "I must get my shoulders square to the target" [55:29]. Fault:
  jump-then-turn; correct: turn-then-jump [55:38]. His check is from under
  the basket [55:47]. HE NEVER MENTIONS TOE DIRECTION.
E19's "dominant foot forward is NOT Magee", "power from the legs has no Magee
source" and "no stance width" are FALSE. domfoot.txt v1 was written on a wrong
premise (it was also reverted for gaming the metric, which stands).

## Why SQUARE v1 was anti-correlated (r = -0.35)
It compared the foot line to the shoulder line INSIDE the body. That penalises
the normal open stance (feet ~11 o'clock, shoulders square - Curry, Hanlen,
Klay "feet slightly turned"), which the expert scores 8.5-10 (shot-125 got 4),
and rewards a body that is internally consistent but pointed away from where
the ball goes (shot-208, expert 3-5, got 9). The expert's reference is the
TARGET, not the torso. Magee and Hopla-via-Klay agree: shoulders square,
feet and hips do not matter.

## The wrong-rim trap (seen in the fixtures)
shot-195, 125, 156, 217, 201, 198, 211: the shooter shoots TOWARD the camera -
the ball grows after release and exits the top of the frame - and the rim
visible behind the shooter is the FAR basket. A model that takes the visible
rim as the target scores a square shooter 4-5 (shot-195 x2, shot-125). In
shot-208 (outdoor, no rim) the ball exits camera-right while the chest faces
the camera: that is the 3-5.
RULE: infer the target from the BALL EXIT VECTOR first. Exits top and GROWS ->
target behind/above the camera, square = chest facing the camera. SHRINKS ->
target away from the camera; a rim behind the shooter may be it. Exits
left/right -> target on that side; a chest facing the camera is NOT square.
Secondary: the extended shooting arm at release. Never use a rim behind the
shooter unless the ball is going away from the camera.

## Left/right mirroring - likely the Dominant Foot -1.08 bias
shot-201: right-hander facing the camera; his right foot is on the frame's
LEFT and is correctly ahead; the model gave 5. Identify the foot from the
shooting arm, never from frame-left/right.

## Visible cues (front / 3/4 camera), robust to a small shooter
- Width: R = outer shoe-silhouette width / shoulder width at the LAST frame
  with both feet down before takeoff, and again at landing. No daylight
  triangle between the shins = narrow. (Ratios are ours, not Magee's.)
- Open to target: shooting toward camera -> both shoulders visible and
  symmetric = square; apparent shoulder width ~70% of front-on, one shoulder
  occluded = turned ~45 deg.
- Wrong foot: the shoe on the shooting-arm side must be nearer the target.
- Turning during the rise: shoulder-line angle at first upward frame vs at
  release; change > ~20 deg = jump-then-turn (shot-198).
- Landing: foot line rotated vs takeoff; feet crossed; lands narrow and
  turned (shot-208). Hanlen's (non-peer-reviewed) NBA numbers: same angle
  65-70% makes, slight turn 39%, big twist 31%.
- Frames: release ~19 of 28; load ~12-17; landing ~22-27 (live 2.5s window).

## Ladders (coaching-sourced anchors; ratios labelled ours)
SQUARE (shoulders vs shot line at release; landing direction):
  9-10 shoulders within ~15 deg of perpendicular to the shot line at release,
       no rotation takeoff->landing; feet turned <=30 deg is NOT a deduction.
  7-8  15-30 deg open but stable, or <=20 deg rotation on landing.
  5-6  30-45 deg open at release, or jump-then-turn that only squares at release.
  3-4  torso 45-90 deg off the shot line, big air twist, lands facing a
       different direction ("hockey stop").
  1-2  side or back to the target on a set shot.
FEET WIDTH (R at last both-feet-down frame, and at landing; take the lower):
  9-10 R 0.75-1.25 | 7-8 0.6-0.75 or 1.25-1.5 | 5-6 0.5-0.6 or 1.5-1.8, or
  collapses before takeoff | 3-4 R < 0.5 (no floor between shins) or > 1.8 |
  1-2 touching/crossed.
DOMINANT FOOT (shooting-side foot from the shooting arm):
  9-10 ahead by 1/4-1 shoe | 7-8 parallel (Williams 2016: no accuracy
  difference; the expert scores everything >=7) | 5-6 lead > 1.5 shoes with
  hips opened, or wrong foot ahead < 1/2 shoe | 3-4 wrong foot clearly ahead
  (shot-198's load) | 1-2 crossed.
FORWARD MOTION AND TOES:
  9-10 heels rise before release, lands on the spot to ~1 shoe ahead
  (Magee: land slightly ahead; Podmenik: elite guards jump 21-49cm toward the
  basket) | 7-8 lands flat on the spot / drift <= half a body width | 5-6
  heels down at release, or lands behind takeoff | 3-4 fall-away, lateral
  drift >= a shoe, toes >= 45 deg off | 1-2 stumble / one-foot landing.

## Literature
Williams et al. 2016: stagger vs parallel, no accuracy difference. Podmenik
2021: 6-55 deg of shoulder rotation DURING PREPARATION is normal; grows with
distance. Stirn 2019: shoulder rotation finishes at release, hips keep
rotating to landing -> rotation AFTER the shoulders should have locked is the
fault. Cabarkapa 2023: proficient FT shooters have less forward trunk lean.
