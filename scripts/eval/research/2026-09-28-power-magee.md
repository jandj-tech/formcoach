# Power chain: what Magee says (little), what the literature says (timing) (2026-09-28)

Agent 3 (CV / biomechanics research). Condensed.

## Magee
Four points: shooting hand, guide hand, LEGS, target - and every trace of
"Legs" is FOOTWORK and landing ("land slightly ahead", "don't follow your
shot"). Nothing on the dip, one- vs two-motion, a set-point pause, knee depth,
or release timing. His words for an arm shot: "push shot" and "hitch" (on
Fultz: "he kind of pushes the ball towards the basket ... not at all does he
finish his stroke"). "Source of Shot Power", "Connected Shot" and "Shot
Pocket" are the product's constructs, not Magee's. Nearest canonical doctrine
is Wissel: "Your legs and shooting arm work together. As your legs go up, your
arm goes up." Throwing from behind the head / shoving "result from not using
your legs for power".

## The signal that survives a small shooter: EVENT ORDER, not depth
Five frames from head-top and ball position: D dip bottom (lowest head),
B ball starts rising, T takeoff (last flat-footed frame), R release, P jump
peak (highest head).
- Leg-driven: B = D or D+1 (ball and body rise together); at R the knees are
  straight and STOPPED (Okazaki 2012: knee 166-168 deg, knee angular velocity
  5-43 deg/s vs elbow 540-740); beyond mid-range R is BEFORE P (head still
  rising at release; CoM vertical velocity at release +0.4 to +1.0 m/s).
- Arm-driven: ball rises while the head is still level or descending; or the
  ball parks at the set point for 2+ frames while the head keeps moving, then
  a separate push; or a long shot released after P (on the way down).
- At the pinned density (start-to-release 0.57-0.85s over ~5-9 frames) a
  1-frame stall is noise; a PAUSE means 2+ stationary frames.
- The catapult check runs on the SET-POINT frame only; near release "ball
  above the head" is true for everyone.

## Knee depth is weak at the top of the range
Pros: knee 112-121 deg, no proficiency difference (Cabarkapa 2022 JFMK);
college: 109.6 vs 108.0 NS. Recreational 3-pt: 94 vs 113 deg (does
discriminate). FT proficiency goes with LOWER knee angular velocity (calmer).
=> The expert grades WHETHER the knees bent; a model grading HOW MUCH is too
harsh. Knees v2's "do not grade absence of a dip as a fault" is right; Power
should say the same and lean on timing.

## Legitimate variation the model must not penalise
High set point reached before takeoff with no dwell (Klay; shot-189: ball
above the hairline, ONE elbow under it, forearm near vertical, legs still
finishing at release - scored 8-10 by the expert). Shallow dip in strong or
older players. Set shot (heels down) with knees extending through the rise.
Release at the peak on a close shot. Small/no dip on a quick catch-and-shoot.
Landing ahead (Magee prefers it). Guide hand on the ball at the set point.
Not-quite-locked elbow at release (experts ~134-138 deg).

## Terminology hazard
The Frontiers 2021 dip paper uses "catapult" for a DIP STYLE (ball to the
thighs). Not the expert's two-arm sling. Do not let the model import it.

## Klay template (checkable)
Small head drop, ball to the hip, both rise together; ball at the forehead in
the same frame the heels leave the floor; ball moving forward one frame after
takeoff; leaves the hand while the head is still rising and the knees are
straight; lands about a shoe ahead.

## Design lesson
Shot Pocket (0% miss) is a single-frame POSITION question. Power and Connected
are SEQUENCE judgements, and the model answers those from a template. Convert
them to named-frame comparisons: locate D, T, R, P, then ask position
questions on those frames. Same pattern as SETPOINT_CHECK, generalised.
