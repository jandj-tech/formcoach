CONNECTED SHOT RUBRIC v1 — is it one continuous rise from the legs to the fingertips, or does it stop and restart?

THIS IS A TIMING CRITERION AND NOTHING ELSE. Do not deduct here for the elbow, the guide hand, the base or the follow-through — each has its own criterion, and charging a fault twice is how a good shot with one flaw ends up scored as a bad shot. "Connected" asks one question: does the motion flow, or does it hitch?

USE THE SAME FIVE FRAMES AS THE POWER GUIDE: D (lowest head), B (ball starts up), T (last flat-footed frame), R (release), P (highest head). Then watch the BALL between B and R: does it keep moving every frame, or does it sit still?

SCORE ANCHORS — land between anchors when the shot sits between them.
  10 — The ball starts up as the body starts up, never sits still on the way, and leaves while the body is still rising. One piece.
  9  — One frame of stillness at the top, everything else continuous.
  8  — A defined set point reached as the feet leave the floor and launched from immediately — the connected two-motion shot.
  7  — A small hitch — the ball still for about two frames — but the legs and the arm still overlap at the release.
  6  — A clear pause at the set point with the legs already straight; the arm finishes on its own; the ball still leaves before the peak.
  5  — The ball starts rising before the head drops or before the knees start to straighten — the arms lead and the legs catch up.
  4  — The legs finish and the head is already coming down before the ball moves forward; or two separate knee bends.
  3  — More than one hitch, or the ball travels BACKWARD toward the ear before it comes forward, breaking the up-and-out path.
  2  — Upper body only, with a token knee flex that does not line up with the ball's rise.
  1  — No lower-body involvement at all.

A ONE-FRAME STALL IS NOT A HITCH. At the frame spacing you are given, a genuinely continuous shot can look like it paused for a single frame. Call a hitch only when the ball is still for two or more frames while the head keeps moving.

WHAT IS NOT A FAULT: a high set point launched from without a park; a shallow dip; a set shot; a fade or a drift (balance criteria); a guide hand still on the ball at the set point.

RETURN NULL WHEN the clip starts after the gather or ends before the release, or the ball is hidden through the rise.

PLAYER-FACING WORDING: tell them to make it one motion — the ball goes up with the legs and leaves before the body stops rising, no pause at the top. Never mention frames, letters, angles or scoring bands in the reasoning.
