ELBOW RUBRIC v7 — is the shooting elbow stacked UNDER the ball, or off to the side of it?

WHAT AN L LOOKS LIKE. Ball over hand over forearm over elbow, in one vertical stack. The forearm points up at the ceiling, the elbow points down at the floor, and the set point sits around FOREHEAD height on the shooting side of the head. That stack is the whole target.

THE PRIMARY MEASUREMENT IS SIDEWAYS OFFSET, AND THE RULER IS THE BALL. Pick the frame where the ball is at its set point — up around the face, still held, not yet released. Drop a vertical line down the centre of the ball. Now find the point of the elbow. How far is the elbow from that line, measured in BALL WIDTHS? The ball is in the same frame, always the same size, and shrinks with the player, so this works on a small distant shooter where degrees and body lines do not.

THE MEASUREMENT DECIDES THE SCORE, AND SOFTENING WORDS MAY NOT RAISE IT. Once
you have the measurement, the anchor for that measurement IS the score. You may
not move it up because the fault looked minor, because the rest of the shot was
good, because the deviation corrected itself, or because the player is clearly
trying.

This is the measured failure mode on this criterion. Every correction the expert
made here was DOWNWARD, and the reasoning that earned them reads the same way
every time:

  "elbow reasonably tucked under the ball, forming a recognizable L-shape …
   a slight outward angle but nothing severe"          scored 8.0 → expert 3.0 (a catapult)
  "the elbow drifts slightly outside the ball line on the way up, which costs
   a little efficiency"                                scored 7.5 → expert 3.0 (ball behind the head)
  "a slight opening of the angle at the set point but it's within a workable
   range"                                              scored 8.0 → expert 6.5

In each of those the deviation was SEEN and then discounted. "Reasonably",
"recognisable", "slight", "minor", "nothing severe", "workable", "a little",
"small natural", "within range" and "not significant" are the signature of that
discount. If you are about to write one of them about something you measured,
stop — the measurement is the score. Report what you measured and let the anchor
set the number.

A player is better served by a 4 that names the real fault than by an 8 that
tells them a real fault is nothing.

SCORE ANCHORS — ALL MEASURED AT THE SET POINT. Land between two anchors when the arm sits between them; every whole and half point from 1 to 10 is available.
  10 — Elbow within about half a ball width of the line down the centre of the ball, forearm reading as one vertical column under it, guide hand resting on the side. This is the band a good shooter lands in on a normal attempt — it is not reserved for a protractor-perfect arm.
  7  — Elbow roughly one ball width outside the line: recognisably stacked, clearly on the shooting side, but visibly off the ball's centre, or drifting out during the rise and recovering by the set point.
  4  — Elbow about two ball widths outside the line: winged out to the side with the ball resting beside the head rather than above the elbow. This is the most common real fault and it is a one-hand shot with a flared elbow — write it as exactly that.
  1  — No stack at any point: the ball never sits above the elbow, the arm pushes it from beside the shoulder, or the elbow is out so far the upper arm is level with the shoulders.

TWO SHAPE FLOORS, EACH NEEDING A FRAME YOU CAN POINT AT. These override the offset anchors:
  - BALL BEHIND THE HEAD (the catapult): a frame where the ball is level with or behind the top of the head with both hands still gripping it and both elbows raised beside the ears, then slung forward. Score 1-3 and say the ball is launched from behind the head.
  - TWO-HAND CHEST SHOT: the ball clearly never loads above the chin and is shoved out from chest height by both arms together, both elbows winged wide. Score 3-4.
  - A SIDEWAYS L — arm bent but rotated so the forearm points sideways rather than up — scores 3-4, the same as an elbow completely out.
IF NEITHER OF THOSE FRAMES EXISTS, NO FLOOR APPLIES: score the sideways offset and use flared-elbow wording. NEVER write catapult, heave, sling, hoist, chest pass or two-hand about a shot that released off one hand. The expert has corrected exactly that: "this was NOT a catapult — this was just the shooting hand being flared out" (he scored that elbow a 4). When torn between a floor and the offset anchors, take the milder one.

THE FOLD ANGLE IS ONLY SCORABLE FROM THE SIDE — READ THIS BEFORE DEDUCTING FOR IT.
  FROM A CAMERA IN FRONT OF THE SHOOTER, including the usual view from under or behind the basket looking back at them, THE FOLD ANGLE AT THE ELBOW IS NOT MEASURABLE. The forearm points toward the camera and is foreshortened, so the angle you appear to see is produced by how the player is turned, not by how the arm is folded. Score the sideways offset alone from this angle and take NOTHING off for the fold. Do not write that the arm looked open, straight, or over-folded from a front view — that is the single most repeatable way this criterion gets two different scores on the same shot.
  FROM A SIDE CAMERA, where you can see the upper arm and the forearm as two separate segments, the fold is readable and adds to the score:
    - Forearm standing under the ball with a working bend, anywhere from about a right angle to somewhat more open: normal, no deduction.
    - THE OPEN PUSH — the arm has plainly given up the bend: upper arm and forearm in a near-straight line and the ball out beside the head rather than above the elbow, so there is no bend left to load the wrist. Cap at 4.
    - THE LAY-BACK — the forearm folded back toward the head instead of standing under the ball, the ball travelling back over the player. Cap at 3, and check the catapult floor.
  A straight arm AT FULL EXTENSION is the finish of a good shot, not an open push. Judge the fold from the rise through the set point only.

SCORE THE REP IN FRONT OF YOU, NOT THE SHOOTER. The same player produces a clean stack on most attempts and a visibly worse one now and then. Do not average an obviously good rep down because nobody is perfect, and do not carry a good early frame forward over a rep where the arm plainly collapsed.

RETURN NULL WHEN: the shooting elbow is never visible in any frame between the rise and the set point, or the only frames you have are at or after the release. Do not score the extension in place of the set point.

EXPERT CALIBRATION:
  - An elite shooter at his set point from the side: ball above the shooting shoulder, forearm under it, elbow pointing down and sitting under the ball, the bend a little shy of a right angle. 9-9.5. Marking this down for not measuring exactly 90, or calling it a V, is the error.
  - Player square to the camera, ball beside his head, elbow well out to the side rather than under the ball, pushing the shot from the side of his body — the expert said this player "did not have an L shape at all": 1-3, not 5 or 6.
  - Player who carried the ball up the middle of his body with both hands mirrored on it, both elbows winged wide, ball riding above and behind his head before whipping out: the catapult floor, a clear failure, never 7-8.
  - Symmetric-looking rise, ball loading in front of the face, then a one-hand release with the shooting elbow flared and the guide hand hanging close: 4, written up as a flared elbow and nothing else.

PLAYER-FACING WORDING: tell them to get the shooting hand under the ball with the elbow pointing at the floor — one vertical forearm carrying the ball at face height, the other hand just along for the ride. Never mention degrees, angles, ball widths, lines or frames in the reasoning.
