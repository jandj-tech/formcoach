ELBOW RUBRIC v10 — where is the shooting elbow relative to the ball, and where is the ball relative to the head?

READ THE ARM AS A SILHOUETTE. Everything here is judged at the SET POINT — the highest frame where the ball is still held, before the arms extend — from the outline of the body, the ball and the arms. None of it needs a measurement in degrees or pixels. If the shooter is small in the frame, these shapes are still readable; a bent-arm angle is not, and you are not asked for one.

THE THREE THINGS TO FIND, IN THIS ORDER:

1. WHERE IS THE BALL RELATIVE TO THE HEAD?
   - IN FRONT of the head, above the forehead, on the shooting side: normal.
   - BESIDE the head, level with the ear, off to the shooting side: the elbow is out — the ball can only sit there if the upper arm has swung outward.
   - OVER THE CROWN or BEHIND the head, both hands still on it: the catapult. Check whether the ball travelled UP AND BACK to get there and paused before coming forward — that path is the fault, and it is visible in the frames before the set point, not at the release.

2. WHERE IS THE ELBOW RELATIVE TO THE BALL AND THE SHOULDER?
   Drop a vertical line through the ball. Then look at the outer edge of the shooting-side shoulder.
   - Elbow INSIDE the ball's outline, under it: stacked.
   - Elbow OUTSIDE the ball but clearly inside the shoulder edge: slightly out.
   - Elbow AT or BEYOND the shoulder edge, upper arm near horizontal: flared.
   - BOTH elbows outside the torso outline, arms making a W or a wide V with the ball in the middle: a two-arm load.

3. HOW DOES THE FOREARM STAND?
   - Standing up under the ball, close to vertical: normal. (Cabarkapa 2021: proficient shooters hold the forearm within about 8 degrees of vertical; the missed shots of good shooters were at 11; non-proficient shooters at 20. You are not measuring this — you are asking whether the forearm looks like a post or like a lean.)
   - Visibly leaning, so the hand is inboard and the elbow outboard: the arm is a V, not an L.
   - Laid back so the hand is behind the elbow: catapult territory — check the ball position again.

THE COACH'S OWN TELL: on the shooting hand you should NOT see the little finger on the side of the ball. If the pinky is visible on the side, the hand is on the side of the ball and the elbow is out. Use it when the hand is readable; when it is not, the three shapes above decide.

SCORE ANCHORS — every whole and half point from 1 to 10 is available. Land between anchors when the shot sits between them. Do not round toward the milder anchor; pick the picture that matches.
  10 — Ball in front of the forehead on the shooting side. Elbow inside the ball's outline. Forearm a post. One hand finishes, the guide hand stays on the side and peels. Pinky hidden.
  9  — As 10 with one small thing: the elbow a touch outside the ball's outline, or a brief drift on the rise that is gone by the set point.
  8  — Elbow outside the ball's outline but well inside the shoulder edge. Forearm still standing. Ball still in front of the head. One-hand finish. A solid ordinary shot.
  7  — Elbow outside the ball and drifting toward the shoulder edge; forearm beginning to lean; ball still in front of the head on the shooting side.
  6  — Elbow about halfway between the ball's outline and the shoulder edge, the upper arm visibly angled out, the ball still rising on the shooting side. Pinky often visible here. THE MOST COMMON REAL SHOT lands at 6 or 7.
  5  — Elbow near the shoulder edge; the ball has moved toward the side of the head; the forearm leans so the hand is inboard of the elbow. Still one hand finishing.
  4  — Elbow AT the shoulder edge, upper arm near horizontal, ball BESIDE the head at ear level. Write it as a flared elbow with the ball beside the head. Still a one-hand shot.
  3  — Elbow beyond the shoulder edge, the arm pushing from the side, no stack at the set point; or the sideways L — bent arm rotated so the forearm points sideways rather than up.
  1-2 — CATAPULT: the ball went up and BACK to sit over the crown or behind the head, paused, and was slung forward, both elbows high and wide, both hands on it. Or the two-hand chest shove: the ball never loads above the chin and both arms push it out together.

WHAT SEPARATES 4 FROM 2. Both have the elbow far out. The difference is the ball and the hands: a 4 keeps the ball in front of or beside the head and releases off ONE hand with the guide hand peeling; a 2 has the ball over or behind the head, or both hands throwing. The expert has corrected the confusion in both directions — "this was NOT a catapult, this was just the shooting hand being flared out" (he scored that elbow 4), and "it was a catapult shot, the ball was in a V at the top and out" (he scored that elbow 3). If a set-point check has been supplied above this guide, its answers decide which side of that line the shot is on.

DO NOT SOFTEN A SHAPE YOU SAW. "Reasonably tucked", "slight outward angle", "nothing severe", "within a workable range" have each been written about shots the expert scored 3. If you saw the elbow at the shoulder edge, the score is 4, however good the follow-through looked afterward. A player is better served by a 4 that names the fault than by an 8 that calls it nothing.

THE FOLD ANGLE IS NOT SCORED FROM THE FRONT. From a camera in front of the shooter the forearm is foreshortened and the bend you appear to see comes from how the player is turned. Judge the three shapes above and take nothing off for the fold. From a side camera, a shooting arm that has plainly given up its bend — upper arm and forearm in a near-straight line with the ball out beside the head — caps at 4 (the open push); a forearm folded back over the player caps at 3 and sends you to the catapult check. A straight arm AT FULL EXTENSION is the finish of a good shot, not an open push.

SCORE THE REP IN FRONT OF YOU. Do not average a clean rep down because nobody is perfect, and do not carry a good early frame over a rep where the arm plainly swung out.

RETURN NULL WHEN the shooting elbow is hidden in every frame between the rise and the set point, or every frame you have is at or after the release.

PLAYER-FACING WORDING: tell them to get the shooting hand under the ball with the elbow pointing at the floor, the ball up in front of the forehead on the shooting side, the other hand only along for the ride. Never mention degrees, angles, lines, pixels, studies or frames in the reasoning.
