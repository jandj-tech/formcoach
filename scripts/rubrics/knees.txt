KNEES BENT RUBRIC v2 — did the legs load before the shot went up?

WHY THIS REPLACES THE POINTS-SUM VERSION. The previous rubric split this
criterion into three sub-scores worth 4, 3 and 2 points, summed them and scaled
by (pts ÷ 9) × 10. Its top bands read "4 = elite athletic load", "3 = fully
controlled", "2 = explosive and efficient". The arithmetic that produces is
brutal: a good-but-not-elite shooter earning 3 + 2 + 1.5 lands on 6.5/9 = 7.2,
and an ordinary competent one earning 2.5 + 1.5 + 1 lands on 5/9 = 5.6.

Measured against the expert's own labels, this criterion missed 14 times out of
14 and EVERY SINGLE MISS WAS TOO LOW — by 1.0 to 4.0 points, averaging 2.1. The
scores it produced were 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 against expected bands of [7, 9] and
[8, 10]. It was not misreading the footage; it was reserving its top marks for
professional athletes while the expert was grading schoolchildren on whether
they bent their knees at all.

So: no sub-points, no scaling. One judgement, scored directly, with the top of
the scale reachable by a normal player doing the thing correctly.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING FOR. A dip. Somewhere between the gather and the start of
the rise the knees should visibly flex and then extend to drive the shot upward.
That is the whole target. It is not depth for its own sake, it is not squatting,
and it is emphatically not athleticism — a small player with a modest, smooth
dip who pushes up off it has done this correctly and belongs at the top of the
scale.

THE RULER IS THE PLAYER'S OWN HEAD. Measure the dip as how far the crown of the
head drops between standing and the lowest point of the gather, in HEAD HEIGHTS.
The head stays readable when the shooter is small and far away, and it shrinks
with them, so half a head is half a head at any distance. Never in inches or
degrees.

JUDGE IT BETWEEN THE GATHER AND THE START OF THE RISE. If the clip begins at or
after the release, the evidence is not present — say so and return null. Do not
infer a knee bend from a completed jump.

SCORE ANCHORS — every whole and half point is available. Most players who bend
their knees at all belong at 7 or above; the expert's own scores on this
criterion sit in the 7-to-10 range and a 5 should feel like a real finding.
  10 — A clear dip of roughly a third of a head height or more, smooth, both knees together, flowing straight into the upward drive with no pause. Normal good form, not elite form.
  9  — A clear dip that flows into the rise, with one small blemish: slightly uneven knees, or a brief hitch between the load and the drive.
  8  — A visible dip of about a quarter of a head, used to push upward. This is the ordinary competent shot and it belongs here, NOT at 5 or 6.
  7  — A shallow but real dip — an eighth of a head or so — or a good dip that the player does not fully convert into upward drive.
  6  — Barely perceptible knee flex: the legs are doing something but the shot is mostly arms.
  5  — No usable dip. The player shoots from a standing position with straight or near-straight legs. This is a genuine fault and should be rare.
  3  — Straight legs throughout AND the body working against itself, leaning back or sideways because the legs contributed nothing.
  1  — No lower-body involvement at any point, or the knees collapse inward or buckle under the load.

DO NOT DEDUCT FOR ANY OF THESE. They are the reasons the old rubric scored too
low, and each one is either correct form or a different criterion's business:
  - A shallow dip on a close-range or quick-release shot. Shooters load less
    when the shot needs less.
  - A dip that looks unathletic, stiff or slow. This criterion asks whether the
    knees bent, not whether the player is a good athlete.
  - Lack of visible explosiveness or "elite" loading. There is no such band.
  - Feet leaving the floor, or not leaving the floor. Not this criterion.
  - Balance, lean or landing position. Those belong to the base and
    forward-motion criteria.

PLAYER-FACING WORDING: tell them what their legs did and, if there is a fault,
that the shot should start from a bend and push up through the legs. Never
mention points, sub-scores, head heights, fractions or any number.
