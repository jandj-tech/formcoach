SQUARE RUBRIC v6 — do the toes and the shoulders point the same way?

YOU DO NOT NEED TO SEE THE RIM. This criterion asks whether the player's own body agrees with itself. Never return null because the basket is out of frame, and never skip a deduction because you could not confirm where it is.

HOW TO SEE FOOT DIRECTION FROM THE USUAL CAMERA. Filmed from under or behind the basket looking back at the shooter, a foot pointed at the camera shows you its TOP — the laces, the toe box, the shoe at its shortest. A foot rotated toward sideways shows you its SIDE — the full length of the sole, the shoe at its longest. That contrast is what you are reading, and it is clearer from this angle than from any other. From a SIDE camera it reverses: a squared foot shows its side and a turned foot shows its top or heel. Say in your reasoning which of the two views you had.

THE MEASUREMENT DECIDES THE SCORE, AND SOFTENING WORDS MAY NOT RAISE IT. Once
you have the measurement, the anchor for that measurement IS the score. You may
not move it up because the fault looked minor, because the rest of the shot was
good, because the deviation corrected itself, or because the player is clearly
trying.

This is the measured failure mode on this criterion. Every correction the expert
made here was DOWNWARD, and the reasoning that earned them reads the same way
every time:

  "your feet, hips and shoulders are all aimed toward the basket with only a
   small natural offset — you're well squared up"      scored 8.0 → expert 4.5
   (the expert: "feet are way offset to one side … your body is facing one way
   whilst your feet are going the other")
  "all aimed in the same direction throughout the shot — no twisting or
   fighting your own base"                             scored 9.0 → expert 6.0
   (the expert: "he is square to start with, however, when he shoots, he twists
   his whole body")
  "upper body, hips and feet all appear aligned along the same shot line"
                                                       scored 9.0 → expert 4.0

NOTE WHAT THE SECOND ONE MEANS: "square throughout the shot" was asserted about
a shot that visibly twisted. Alignment at the set point is not the test — the
test runs from the rise to the landing, and a base that swings round to get the
shot away fails it however square it started.

In each of those the deviation was SEEN and then discounted. "Reasonably",
"recognisable", "slight", "minor", "nothing severe", "workable", "a little",
"small natural", "within range" and "not significant" are the signature of that
discount. If you are about to write one of them about something you measured,
stop — the measurement is the score. Report what you measured and let the anchor
set the number.

A player is better served by a 4 that names the real fault than by an 8 that
tells them a real fault is nothing.

TWO CHECKS, AND THE SECOND ONE IS THE ONE THIS CRITERION KEEPS MISSING.

  CHECK A — AT THE SET POINT. Do the toes and the shoulders show the same face
  to the camera? This is the static question, and it is the one that gets
  answered.

  CHECK B — THROUGH THE RELEASE AND THE LANDING. Does that alignment HOLD, or
  does the body twist to get the shot away? Compare the set-point frame with
  the release frame and with the landing. A player can be perfectly square at
  the set point and still fail this criterion by winding the torso round as
  they rise.

CHECK B IS NOT OPTIONAL AND IT IS NOT A TIE-BREAKER. Half of the expert's
corrections on this criterion are Check B failures that were scored as if only
Check A existed. One reads, verbatim: "he is square to start with, however,
when he shoots, he twists his whole body. take that into account as well." That
shot was scored 9 and the expert gave it 6. Writing "square throughout the
shot" after looking at a single frame is the specific error being corrected —
you cannot report on "throughout" from one moment.

THE MEASURED FAILURE: THIS CRITERION NEVER SCORES BELOW 7. Across every
correction the expert has made here the AI produced 7, 8 or 9 and nothing else,
while the expert's own scores were 4, 4.5, 6 and 9. Three of the four were
corrected DOWNWARD by 2.5 to 5 points. This criterion has the largest average
error of any in the rubric — a median of 3.5 points, with 60% of its misses
more than 2 points out — and the reason is that the bottom half of its scale is
never used. If your answer is 7 or above, you must be able to say which check
passed and that BOTH did.

SCORE ANCHORS — every whole and half point is available, and the 3-to-6 band is
where a genuinely misaligned shot belongs.
  10 — Both feet show the same face to the camera as each other, and the SHOULDERS show the same face as the feet: toes, hips and shoulders all aimed one way, the shot coming out along that line, and it HOLDS from the set point through the landing. A pair of toes turned a little off the shot line is INCLUDED here — a right-hander pointing slightly left, a left-hander slightly right, is a stance coaches teach on purpose. So is a few degrees of settling or shuffle between the gather and the landing.
  9  — As above, and the expert's own wording for this band is "whole body and feet are pointed in the same direction with no rotation happening in the body". A shot he scored 9 after the AI had said 7 — do not be stingy at the top when both checks genuinely pass.
  8  — Aligned at the set point and holds, with one small imperfection: a slight settle, or the two feet a few degrees off each other while the shoulders match one of them.
  7  — The feet visibly move during the shot — a pivot or a step round as the player rises — but land pointing where the shoulders point, and the upper body never has to work around them. Also 7 when the two feet disagree with each other while the shoulders still match one of them.
  6  — CHECK A PASSES AND CHECK B FAILS: square at the set point, then the torso visibly twists to get the shot away. This is the expert's score for exactly that shot. The base was never wrong; the shot rotated off it.
  5  — Mild mismatch at the set point AND some twist through the release: neither check is clean, but the player is still shooting roughly along their base.
  4  — The feet and the shoulders show DIFFERENT faces to the camera at the set point: the shoes presenting their sides while the chest presents its front, or the reverse. The player is shooting across their own base, and the torso is visibly wound round relative to the hips and feet. ALSO 4 to 4.5 when the feet are planted well out to one side of the shot line and the body has to rotate to compensate — the expert's phrasing is "feet are way offset to one side, you can see that your body is facing one way whilst your feet are going the other".
  3  — As 4, and the compensation is severe enough to distort the shooting arm as well — the expert on such a shot: "she has to rotate her body and put her elbow in a weird position to shoot".
  1  — The feet are close to sideways relative to the shot, the whole torso has rotated to get the shot away, and the base visibly swings back round during or after the release.

THE FLAW IS A MISMATCH, NOT AN ANGLE. Foot direction on its own is not the fault and a small amount of foot movement is not the fault. What you are scoring is whether the upper body and the feet disagree — either at the set point (Check A) or by the body twisting off a good base (Check B). Small settles and shuffles stay at 7 and above. A visible torso twist to get the shot away does NOT: that is a 6, even when the base itself was perfect, because the expert scored exactly that shot a 6.

A STAGGERED STANCE IS NOT A TURNED STANCE. The shooting-side foot being slightly AHEAD of the other is correct form and belongs to a different criterion. What matters is the direction the toes POINT, not which foot is forward.

WATCH THE FEET ACROSS FRAMES, NOT IN ONE. One picture of turned feet is just a stance. Compare the gather frame with the rise and the landing. A few degrees of shuffle between them is nothing; a base that has visibly swung to a different direction is the deduction.

THE MOST COMMON ERROR IS SCORING THIS OFF THE UPPER BODY ALONE. Square shoulders and clean arms read as "square" at a glance and that glance never looked at the feet. Look at the shoes every time, and state in your reasoning which face of the shoe you could see. If you cannot say, you have not checked.

RETURN NULL only when the feet are not visible at all during the shooting motion. Not seeing the basket is never a reason.

EXPERT CALIBRATION:
  - Mid-shot with the ball at the set point, upper body and arms aimed one way, both feet clearly planted pointing another, torso visibly rotated back to compensate, rim not in frame: 5-6. Scoring it high because the arms looked fine, or returning null because the basket was out of shot, are both the error.
  - Right-handed shooter whose toes point somewhat left of the shot line, shoulders and arms comfortably along that line, shot coming out clean: 9-10. A coached open stance with nothing to fix; deducting for foot direction alone is the error.
  - The same shooter, feet also shifting a little between the gather and the landing while the shot is unaffected: still 9-10. Dropping this to a 6 or 7 for a small adjustment is the error.

PLAYER-FACING WORDING: describe what you saw the body do — the shoulders having to twist back, or the base swinging round to get the shot away — not the angle of the feet. Never tell a player whose open stance is working that they should point their toes straight, and never make a note of a small foot adjustment. Never mention lines, angles, degrees or frames in the reasoning.
